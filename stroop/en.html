What is the Stroop effect?

When the word "RED" is printed in blue ink, your brain reads the word before it registers the colour — and answering "blue" takes noticeably longer than it should.

That slowdown, when meaning and colour conflict, is the Stroop effect, first described by the psychologist John Ridley Stroop in 1935. It is one of the most reproduced findings in cognitive psychology.

The test measures how well you can override that automatic reading response — what psychologists call inhibitory control — along with sustained attention.

How to play

  • A colour word appears on screen, printed in a different colour.
  • Tap the button for the colour the word is printed in, not the colour it names.
  • Score as many as you can in sixty seconds. Wrong answers cost you time, so stay calm and look only at the colour.

Tip: narrow your eyes slightly and treat the word as a block of colour rather than something to read — most people get noticeably faster.
